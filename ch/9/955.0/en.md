## Chapter 1 General Provisions

#### Art. 1

**Subject matter**[^1-1]

This Act regulates the combating of money laundering as defined in Article 305<sup>bis</sup> of the Swiss Criminal Code (SCC)[^1-2], the combating of terrorist financing as defined in Article 260<sup>quinquies</sup> paragraph 1 SCC, and the due diligence required in financial transactions, including that required to prevent violations of compulsory measures under the Embargo Act of 22 March 2002[^1-3] (EmbA).

[^1-1]: Amended by Annex No 11 of the FA of 26 Sept. 2025 on the Transparency of Legal Persons and the Identification of Beneficial Owners, in force since 1 Oct. 2026 ([AS 2026 323](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2026/323); [BBl 2024 1607](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/fga/2024/1607)).
[^1-2]: [SR 311.0](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/vocabulary/legal-taxonomy/6196)
[^1-3]: [SR 946.231](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/vocabulary/legal-taxonomy/11413)

#### Art. 2

**Scope of application**

<sup>1</sup>

This Act applies to:

- a. financial intermediaries;
- b. natural persons and legal entities that deal in goods commercially and in doing so accept cash (dealers);
- c.[^2-1] advisers.[^2-2]

<sup>2</sup>

Financial intermediaries are:

- a.[^2-3] banks as defined in Article 1*a* of the Banking Act of 8 November 1934[^2-4] (BankA) and the persons defined in Article 1*b* BankA;
- a<sup>bis</sup>.[^2-5] portfolio managers and trustees as defined in Article 2 paragraph 1 letters a and b of the Financial Institutions Act of 15 June 2018[^2-6] (FinIA);
- b.[^2-7] fund management companies as defined in Article 2 paragraph 1 letter d FinIA;
- b<sup>bis</sup>.[^2-8] licensees in accordance with Article 13 paragraph 2 letters b–d the Collective Investment Schemes Act of 23 June 2006[^2-9], (CISA) and the managers of collective assets in accordance with Article 2 paragraph 1 letter c FinIA;
- c.[^2-10] insurance institutions as defined in the Insurance Supervision Act of 17 December 2004[^2-11] that deal in direct life insurance or offer or distribute shares in collective investment schemes;
- d.[^2-12] securities firms in accordance with Article 2 paragraph 1 letter e FinIA;
- d<sup>bis</sup>.[^2-13] central counterparties and central securities depositories in accordance with the Financial Market Infrastructure Act of 19 June 2015[^2-14] (FinMIA);
- d<sup>ter</sup>.[^2-15] payment systems that require authorisation from the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) in accordance with Article 4 paragraph 2 FinMIA;
- d<sup>quater</sup>.[^2-16] trading facilities for DLT securities in accordance with Article 73*a* FinMIA (DLT trading facilities);
- e.[^2-17] casinos as defined in the Gambling Act of 29 September 2017[^2-18] (GamblA);
- f.[^2-19] promoters of large-scale games under the GamblA;
- g.[^2-20] trade assayers and group companies in accordance with Article 42<sup>bis</sup> of the Precious Metals Control Act of 20 June 1933[^2-21] (PMCA).

<sup>3</sup>

Financial intermediaries are also persons who on a professional basis accept or hold on deposit assets belonging to others or who assist in the investment or transfer of such assets; they include in particular persons who:

- a. carry out credit transactions (in particular in relation to consumer loans or mortgages, factoring, commercial financing or financial leasing);
- b. provide services related to payment transactions, in particular by carrying out electronic transfers on behalf of other persons, or who issue or manage means of payment such as credit cards and travellers’ cheques;
- c. trade for their own account or for the account of others in banknotes and coins, money market instruments, foreign exchange, precious metals, commodities and securities (stocks and shares and value rights) as well as their derivatives;
- d.[^2-22] ...
- e.[^2-23] ...
- f. make investments as investment advisers;
- g. hold securities on deposit or manage securities.

<sup>3bis</sup>

Advisers are natural and legal persons who, on a professional basis, assist third parties with financial transactions, including raising finance, in connection with the following specific legal acts:

- a. the purchase and sale of immovable property;
- b. the creation and formation of non-operating legal entities with registered office in Switzerland or of legal entities with registered office abroad;
- c. the management and administration of non-operating legal entities;
- d. deposits and distributions from non-operating legal entities;
- e. the purchase and sale of legal entities, provided that the purchase or sale is carried out by a non-operating legal entity.[^2-24]

<sup>3ter</sup> Natural and legal persons who, on a professional basis and for a period of more than six months, provide addresses or premises as a domicile or registered office for legal entities are also regarded as advisers.[^2-25]

<sup>4</sup>

This Act does not apply to:

- a. the Swiss National Bank;
- b. tax-exempt occupational pension institutions;
- c. persons who provide their services solely to tax-exempt occupational pension institutions;
- d. financial intermediaries within the meaning of paragraph 3 who provide their services solely to financial intermediaries within the meaning of paragraph 2 or to foreign financial intermediaries who are subject to equivalent supervision;
- e.[^2-26] limited qualified investor funds (L-QIF) in the legal form of an investment company with variable capital (SICAV) or a limited partnership for collective investment (LPCI) if the institution responsible for management in accordance with Article 118*h* paragraph 1, 2 or 4 CISA assumes responsibility for complying with the duties under this Act;
- f.[^2-27] lawyers and notaries who act in connection with court, criminal, administrative or arbitration proceedings, including providing representation in proceedings and providing advice in connection with the preparation and conduct of proceedings, the clarification of the facts, the assessment of litigation risks, the prevention of such proceedings and the enforcement of the outcomes of proceedings.

<sup>4bis</sup> Natural and legal persons authorised or supervised by the Federal Audit Oversight Authority are not regarded as advisers under this Act in respect of their activities as auditors.[^2-28]

<sup>4ter</sup>

In view of the low risk of money laundering and terrorist financing, this Act does not apply to:

- a. transactions relating to immovable property and legal entities arising from family, marital and matrimonial property law, succession law or gifts, or where the parties involved are related persons within the meaning of Article 2 paragraph 2 letter a FinIA;
- b. transfers of immovable property and legal entities with a value of less than five million francs, provided that the purchase price is paid and received exclusively through banks or other financial intermediaries subject to this Act;
- c. the purchase of residential properties in Switzerland for personal use, or the purchase of residential properties in Switzerland which serve as replacement properties within the meaning of Article 12 paragraph 3 letter e of the Tax Harmonisation Act of 14 December 1990[^2-29];
- d. transfers of farms or immovable property in accordance with the Federal Act of 4 October 1991[^2-30] on Rural Land Rights to persons who intend to farm the property themselves;
- e. transfers of immovable property for the purposes of land consolidation and similar procedures;
- f. services provided to operating legal entities, as well as to charitable foundations and operating associations with registered office in Switzerland;
- g. the establishment of foundations by reason of death;
- h. the simple certification of documents without any related advisory services.[^2-31]

<sup>5</sup> The Federal Council may, by ordinance, provide for further exceptions to Article 2 paragraphs 3<sup>bis</sup> and 3<sup>ter</sup>.[^2-32]

#### Art. 2a

**Definitions**[^2a-1]

<sup>1</sup>

Politically exposed persons in terms of this Act are:

- a. individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country, such as heads of state or of government, senior politicians at national level, senior government, judicial, military or political party officials at national level, and senior executives of state-owned corporations of national significance (foreign politically exposed persons);
- b. individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions at national level in Switzerland in politics, government, the armed forces or the judiciary, or who are or have been senior executives of state-owned corporations of national significance (domestic politically exposed persons);
- c. individuals who are or have been entrusted with a prominent function by an intergovernmental organisation or international sports federations, such as secretaries general, directors, deputy directors and members of the board or individuals who have been entrusted with equivalent functions, (politically exposed persons in international organisations)

<sup>2</sup> The family members and close associates of politically exposed persons are individuals who are closely connected to persons under paragraph 1 either through their family or for social or professional reasons.

<sup>3</sup> The beneficial owners of an operating legal entity are the natural persons who ultimately control the legal entity in that they directly or indirectly, alone or in concert with third parties, hold at least 25 per cent of the capital or voting rights in the legal entity or otherwise control it. If the beneficial owners cannot be identified, the most senior member of the legal entity’s executive body must be identified.

<sup>4</sup> Domestic politically exposed persons are no longer regarded as being politically exposed in terms of this Act when 18 months have elapsed since they relinquished their position. The general duties of due diligence for financial intermediaries are reserved.

<sup>5</sup> An international sports federation in terms of paragraph 1 letter c is the International Olympic Committee and the non-governmental organisations that it recognised that regulate one or more official sports at global level.

<sup>6</sup> Non-operating legal entities are legal persons, companies, institutions, foundations, trusts, fiduciary companies and similar arrangements that are not established or maintained for the purpose of operating or supporting the operational activities of a undertaking or a group, in particular domiciliary companies.[^2a-2]

#### Art. 2b

**Coordination of substantive law**[^2b-1]

<sup>1</sup> Where an activity is classified as both financial intermediation and advice under Article 2 paragraph 3<sup>bis</sup> or 3<sup>ter</sup>, the provisions applicable to financial intermediaries apply to that activity.

<sup>2</sup> Any person who acts both as a financial intermediary and as an adviser is subject to the provisions applicable to each of these activities. They may declare that all their activities are subject to the provisions applicable to financial intermediaries. The Federal Council shall regulate the details of the declaration.

### Section 1 Financial Intermediaries Duty of Due Diligence

[^chap2-sec1-1]

[^chap2-sec1-1]: Amended by No I 7 of the FA of 12 Dec. 2014 on the Implementation of the 2012 revision of the Recommendations of the Financial Action Task Force, in force since 1 Jan. 2016 ([AS 2015 1389](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2015/289); [BBl 2014 605](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/fga/2014/100)).

#### Art. 3

**Verification of the identity of the customer**

<sup>1</sup> When establishing a business relationship, the financial intermediary must verify the identity of the customer on the basis of a document of evidentiary value. Where the customer is a legal entity, the financial intermediary must acknowledge the provisions regulating the power to bind the legal entity, and verify the identity of the persons who enter into the business relationship on behalf of the legal entity.[^3-1]

<sup>2</sup> In the case of cash transactions with a customer whose identity has not yet been identified, the duty to verify identity applies only if one transaction, or two or more transactions that appear to be connected, involve a considerable financial value.

<sup>3</sup> Insurance institutions must verify the identity of the customer if the amount of a single premium, the regular premium or the total of the premiums involves a considerable financial value.

<sup>4</sup> If in cases under paragraphs 2 or 3 there is any suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, the identity of the customer must be verified even if the relevant amounts have not been reached.[^3-2]

<sup>5</sup> FINMA, the Federal Gaming Board (FGB), the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP), the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS)[^3-3] and the self-regulatory organisations shall determine what constitutes a considerable financial value within the meaning of paragraphs 2 and 3 in their respective fields and adjust such values as required.[^3-4]

#### Art. 4

**Establishing the identity of the beneficial owner**[^4-1]

<sup>1</sup> The financial intermediary must identify the beneficial owner with the due diligence required in the circumstances and verify its identity in order to ensure that it knows who the beneficial owner is.[^4-2] If the customer is a listed company or a subsidiary over which a listed company has majority control, the identity of the beneficial owner need not be established.

<sup>2</sup>

The financial intermediary must obtain a written declaration, or another form of declaration that may be evidenced in text, from the customer as to the identity of the individual who is the beneficial owner if:[^4-3]

- a. the customer is not the beneficial owner or if there is any doubt about the matter;
- b. the customer is a domiciliary company or an operating legal entity; or
- c. a cash transaction of considerable financial value in terms of Article 3 paragraph 2 is being carried out.

<sup>3</sup> In the case of collective accounts or collective deposits, the financial intermediary must require the customer to provide a complete list of the beneficial owners and to give notice of any change to the list immediately.

#### Art. 5

**Repetition of the verification of the identity of the customer or the establishment of the identity of the beneficial owner**

<sup>1</sup> If doubt arises in the course of the business relationship as to the identity of the customer or of the beneficial owner, the verification of identity or establishment of identity in terms of Articles 3 and 4 respectively must be repeated.

<sup>2</sup> In the case of an insurance policy that may be surrendered, the insurance institution must also re-establish the identity of the beneficial owner if, in the event of a claim or the surrender of the policy, the person entitled to benefit is not the same person identified at the time that the insurance contract was concluded.

#### Art. 6

**Special duties of due diligence**[^6-1]

<sup>1</sup> The financial intermediary is required to ascertain the nature and purpose of the business relationship wanted by the customer. The extent of the information that must be obtained, the hierarchical level at which the decision to enter into or continue a business relationship must be taken and the regularity of checks are determined by the risk represented by the customer.

<sup>2</sup>

The financial intermediary must clarify the economic background and the purpose of a transaction or of a business relationship if:

- a. the transaction or the business relationship appears unusual, unless its legality is clear;
- b.[^6-2] there are indications that assets are the proceeds of a felony or an aggravated tax misdemeanour under Article 305<sup>bis</sup> number 1<sup>bis</sup> SCC[^6-3] or are subject to the power of disposal of a criminal or terrorist organisation (Art. 260<sup>ter</sup> SCC) or serve the financing of terrorism (Art. 260<sup>quinquies</sup> para. 1 SCC);
- c. the transaction or the business relationship carries a higher risk;
- d.[^6-4] the data on a customer, a beneficial owner or an authorised signatory in a business relationship or transaction are identical or very similar to the data passed on to the financial intermediary under Article 22*a* paragraph 2 or 3.

<sup>3</sup> Business relationships with foreign politically exposed persons and their family members or close associates in terms of Article 2*a* paragraph 2 are deemed in every case to be business relationships with a higher risk.

<sup>4</sup> Business relationships with domestic politically exposed persons and politically exposed persons in international organisations and their family members or close associates in terms of Article 2*a* paragraph 2 are deemed when combined with one or more further risk criteria to be business relationships with a higher risk.

#### Art. 7

**Duty to keep records**

<sup>1</sup> The financial intermediary must keep records of transactions carried out and of clarifications required under this Act in such a manner that other specially qualified persons are able to make a reliable assessment of the transactions and business relationships and of compliance with the provisions of this Act.

<sup>1bis</sup> The financial intermediary must periodically check the required records to ensure that they are up to date, and update them if need be. The periodicity, scope and type of checking and updating are based on the risk posed by the customer.[^7-1]

<sup>2</sup> The financial intermediary must retain the records in such a manner as to be able to respond within a reasonable time to any requests made by the prosecution authorities for information or for the seizure of assets.

<sup>3</sup> After the termination of the business relationship or after completion of the transaction, the financial intermediary must retain the records for a minimum of ten years.

### Section 2 Duties in the Event of a Suspicion of Money Laundering

#### Art. 9

**Duty to report**

<sup>1</sup>

A financial intermediary must immediately file a report with the Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (the Reporting Office) as defined in Article 23 if it:

- a.

  knows or has reasonable grounds to suspect that assets involved in the business relationship:

  - 1\.[^9-1] are connected to an offence in terms of Article 260<sup>ter</sup> or 305<sup>bis</sup> SCC[^9-2],
  - 2\.[^9-3] are the proceeds of a felony or an aggravated tax misdemeanour under Article 305<sup>bis</sup> number 1<sup>bis</sup> SCC,
  - 3\.[^9-4] are subject to the power of disposal of a criminal or terrorist organisation, or
  - 4\. serve the financing of terrorism (Art. 260<sup>quinquies</sup> para. 1 SCC);

- b. terminates negotiations aimed at establishing a business relationship because of a reasonable suspicion as defined in letter a;
- c.[^9-5] knows or has reason to assume based on the clarifications carried out under Article 6 paragraph 2 letter d that the data on a person or organisation passed on under Article 22*a* paragraph 2 or 3 correspond to the data of a customer, a beneficial owner or an authorised signatory in a business relationship or transaction.[^9-6]

<sup>1bis</sup>

A dealer must immediately file a report with the Reporting Office if it knows or has reasonable grounds to suspect that cash payments made in the course of a commercial transaction:

- a.[^9-7] are connected to an offence under Article 260<sup>ter</sup> or 305<sup>bis</sup> SCC;
- b. are the proceeds of a felony or an aggravated tax misdemeanour under Article 305<sup>bis</sup> number 1<sup>bis</sup> SCC;
- c.[^9-8] are subject to the power of disposal of a criminal organisation; or
- d.[^9-9] serve the financing of terrorism (Art. 260<sup>quinquies</sup> para. 1 SCC).[^9-10]

<sup>1ter</sup>

An adviser must immediately file a report with the Reporting Office if they:

- a.

  know or have reasonable grounds to suspect that the assets involved in the transaction or service:

  - 1\. are connected with an offence under Article 260<sup>ter</sup> or 305<sup>bis</sup> SCC,
  - 2\. are the proceeds of a felony or an aggravated tax misdemeanour under Article 305<sup>bis</sup> number 1<sup>bis</sup> SCC,
  - 3\. are subject to the power of disposal of a criminal or terrorist organisation, or
  - 4\. serve the financing of terrorism (Art. 260<sup>quinquies</sup> para. 1 SCC);

- b. terminate negotiations in relation to their services because of a reasonable suspicion as defined in letter a;
- c. know or have reason to assume based on the clarifications carried out under Article 8*b* paragraph 3 that the data on a person or organisation passed on under Article 22 *a* paragraph 2 correspond to the data of a customer, a beneficial owner or an authorised signatory in a business relationship, transaction or service.[^9-11]

<sup>1quater</sup> The name of the financial intermediary, dealer or adviser must appear in any report filed in accordance with paragraphs 1, 1<sup>bis</sup> or 1<sup>ter</sup>. The identity of the financial intermediary’s, dealer’s or adviser’s staff who are in charge of the case may be made anonymous in the report, provided it is guaranteed that the Reporting Office and the competent prosecution authority are able to contact them without delay.[^9-12]

<sup>1quinquies</sup> In the cases referred to in paragraph 1, reasonable grounds to suspect exist if the financial intermediary has specific evidence or several indications that paragraph 1 letter a may apply to the assets involved in the business relationship and this suspicion cannot be dispelled on the basis of additional clarifications in accordance with Article 6.[^9-13]

<sup>1sexies</sup> In the cases referred to in paragraphs 1<sup>bis</sup> and 1<sup>ter</sup>, the definition of ‘reasonable suspicion’ set out in paragraph 1<sup>quinquies</sup> applies *mutatis mutandis*.[^9-14]

<sup>2</sup>

Any person acting as a lawyer or notary is only obliged to file a report if the following conditions are met:

- a. they are carrying out a financial transaction in the name of or on behalf of a customer;
- b. the information in their possession is not protected by professional confidentiality in accordance with Article 321 SCC.[^9-15]

#### Art. 18a

**Preserving professional confidentiality**[^18a-1]

<sup>1</sup> In order to preserve professional confidentiality, self-regulatory organisations shall arrange for inspections under this Act (AMLA inspections) to be carried out on lawyers by lawyers and on notaries by notaries.

<sup>2</sup>

The lawyers and notaries instructed to carry out AMLA inspections must meet the following requirements:

- a. lawyer’s or notary’s practising certificate;
- b. guarantee of that inspections will be carried out properly;
- c. proof of the relevant knowledge of AMLA, practical experience and continuing professional development;
- d. independence from the person affiliated to the self-regulatory organisation who is being inspected.

<sup>3</sup> Where there are objective grounds to suspect a violation of the duty of due diligence, it is absolutely necessary for the purposes of the inspections, and professional confidentiality has been waived by a court or by the customer, lawyers and notaries must disclose information subject to professional confidentiality to the lawyers and notaries responsible for carrying out the AMLA inspections. The self-regulatory organisations shall lay down objective criteria for deciding whether duties of due diligence have been violated.

<sup>4</sup> Subject to paragraph 3, lawyers and notaries responsible for carrying out AMLA inspections shall not have access to information protected by professional confidentiality. They shall not disclose information made available following the lifting of professional confidentiality to either the self-regulatory organisations or other authorities, and must not include such information in any report to the self-regulatory organisation on the inspection. They shall ensure that information and documents obtained during the inspection are held in safekeeping and that professional confidentiality is maintained.

<sup>5</sup> The compulsory measures court at the place where the adviser is based has jurisdiction to lift professional confidentiality.

<sup>6</sup> Where a notary is also subject to supervision by a cantonal authority pursuant to cantonal law, and the appointed person meets both the requirements set out in paragraph 2 and those of cantonal law, the supervision may be coordinated.

### Section 2 Cooperation with Foreign Authorities

#### Art. 30

**Cooperation with foreign reporting offices**[^30-1]

<sup>1</sup>

The Reporting Office may pass on the personal data and other information that are in its possession or that it may obtain under this Act to a foreign reporting office provided that office:

- a. guarantees that it will use the information solely for the purpose of analysis in the context of combating money laundering and its predicate offences, organised crime or terrorist financing;
- b. guarantees that it will reciprocate on receipt of a similar request from Switzerland;
- c. guarantees that official secrecy and professional confidentiality will be preserved;
- d. guarantees that it will not pass on the information received to third parties without the express consent of the Reporting Office; and
- e. will comply with the conditions and restrictions imposed by the Reporting Office.

<sup>2</sup>

It may pass on the following information in particular:

- a.[^30-2] the name of the financial intermediary, the dealer or the adviser, provided the anonymity is preserved of the person making the report or who has complied with a duty to provide information under this Act;
- b. account holders, account numbers and account balances;
- c. beneficial owners;
- d. details of transactions.

<sup>3</sup> Information is passed on in the form of a report.

<sup>4</sup>

The Reporting Office may consent to information being passed on by the foreign reporting office to a third authority provided the latter guarantees that:

- a.

  it will use the information solely:

  - 1\. for the purpose of analysis in the context of combating money laundering and its predicate offences, organised crime or terrorist financing, or
  - 2\. to institute criminal proceedings relating to money laundering and its predicate offences, organised crime or terrorist financing or to obtain evidence in response to a request for mutual assistance relating to such criminal proceedings;

- b. they will not use the information to prosecute offences that are not offences predicate to money laundering under Swiss law;
- c. they will not use the information in evidence; and
- d. they will preserve official secrecy or professional confidentiality.

<sup>5</sup> If the request to pass on the information to a foreign third authority concerns a matter that is the subject of criminal proceedings in Switzerland, the Reporting Office shall first obtain the consent of the public prosecutor’s office responsible for the proceedings.

<sup>6</sup> The Reporting Office is entitled to make more detailed arrangements on the modalities of cooperation with foreign reporting offices.

#### Art. 35a

**Verification**[^35a-1]

<sup>1</sup>

In order to fulfil its duties, the Reporting Office may by means of a computerised access procedure verify whether a person reported or notified to it is listed in any of the following databases:

- a. the National Police Index;
- b. the Central Migration Information System;
- c.[^35a-2] the Register of Criminal Records Information System VOSTRA;
