## Chapter 1 General Provisions

#### Art. 1

**Functions of official cadastral surveying**[^1-1]

Official cadastral surveying:

- a. shall provide the federal authorities, the cantons and the communes, as well as industry and commerce, academic and scientific institutions and the general public with geospatial reference data in accordance with Article 29 paragraph 1 GeoIA on objects located on, above or below the surface of the earth;
- b. ensures the availability of the official geodata required for the creation and maintenance of the land register in accordance with Article 950 CC.

[^1-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 1a

**Relationship with general geoinformation law**[^1a-1]

Official cadastral surveying is governed by the Geoinformation Ordinance of 21 May 2008[^1a-2] (GeoIO), unless there are special provisions in this present Ordinance.

[^1a-1]: Inserted by No I of the O of 21 May 2008 ([AS 2008 2745](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2008/385)). Amended by No I of the O of 23 Nov. 2022, in force since 1 Jan. 2023 ([AS 2022 764](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2022/764)).
[^1a-2]: [SR 510.620](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/2008/389)

#### Art. 2

**Participation of the cantons and consultation of partner organisations**[^2-1]

When preparing technical standards and other federal requirements that fall within the scope of this Ordinance but do not exclusively concern the Federal Administration, the Confederation shall ensure the participation of the cantons and the consultation of partner organisations in an appropriate manner.

[^2-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

## Chapter 2 Content of the Official Cadastral Survey

#### Art. 5

**Components of the official cadastral survey**[^5-1]

<sup>1</sup>

The components of the official cadastral survey are:

- a. the data;
- b. the fixed point and border point markers on the terrain (point markers);
- c. the technical and administrative documents;
- d. the components and principles of the cadastral survey under the previous system;

<sup>2</sup> The DDPS shall regulate the details, in particular the products derived from the official cadastral survey data. Article 7 is reserved.

[^5-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 6

**Geodata model for official cadastral surveying**[^6-1]

<sup>1</sup> The DDPS shall define the requirements for the geodata model for official cadastral surveying, in particular with regard to content, dimensions, accuracy and reliability. The geodata model may have a modular structure.

<sup>2</sup> Cantonal extensions to the geodata model are not permitted.

[^6-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 7

**Land register plan**[^7-1]

<sup>1</sup> The land register plan is an analogue or digital extract from the official cadastral survey data.

<sup>2</sup>

It shall contain as a minimum the data on:

- a. the border points and boundary lines of the land or buildings (Art. 943 para. 1 no 1 CC);
- b. the border points and boundary lines of distinct and permanent rights to immovable property that have been defined in terms of area (Art. 943 para. 1 no 2 CC);
- c. mines (Art. 943 para. 1 no. 3 CC);
- d. areas subject to continuous movement (Art. 660*a* CC).

<sup>3</sup> The minimum content of the plan has the effect of being recorded in the land register (Art. 971-974 CC).

<sup>4</sup> The cantons may, in addition to the official cadastral survey data, require boundaries of easements to be indicated, provided their positions are clearly defined on the ground.

<sup>5</sup> The Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP) and the DDPS shall jointly determine the requirements for the land register plan and other extracts, in particular with regard to content and presentation.

## Chapter 3 Marking

### Section 1 General provisions

#### Art. 11

**Definition and scope**

<sup>1</sup> Marking involves establishing boundaries and placing boundary markers (monumentation).

<sup>2</sup> Marking is required for territorial boundaries, property boundaries and boundaries of distinct and permanent rights over others’ land (easements), provided the latter can be clearly delimited by boundary lines. Article 17 is reserved.[^11-1]

[^11-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 12

**Cantonal law**

The cantons shall enact regulations on the procedure for legally valid marking in accordance with this Ordinance.

### Section 2 Establishing Boundaries

#### Art. 13

**Procedure**

<sup>1</sup> In general, boundaries shall be established by inspecting the location.

<sup>2</sup>

The cantons may specify that boundaries be established on the basis of plans, aerial photographs or other suitable means:

- a.[^13-1] in agricultural and forest land in mountainous and summer pasture areas as defined in the agricultural production register[^13-2], and in unproductive areas;
- b.[^13-3] in the course of an updating process, subject to the agreement of the landowners concerned.

[^13-1]: [SR 912.1](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1999/46)
[^13-2]: Amended by No I of the O of 7 March 2003, in force since 1 April 2003 ([AS 2003 507](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2003/111)).
[^13-3]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 14

**Boundary line**[^14-1]

<sup>1</sup> A valid boundary line between two adjacent border points may be a straight line or an arc.

<sup>2</sup> In the initial survey, or when finalising or updating the survey, a simple boundary line should be aimed for. Existing boundary lines should be improved where possible.

[^14-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 14a

**Elimination of discrepancies**[^14a-1]

Discrepancies between the cadastral survey data and the situation on the terrain or between the land register plan and other official cadastral survey plans shall be resolved ex officio, taking into account Article 668 paragraph 2 of the Swiss Civil Code.

[^14a-1]: Inserted by No I of the O of 21 May 2008 ([AS 2008 2745](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2008/385)). Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

### Section 3 Monumentation

#### Art. 15

**Principle**

Boundary markers shall be placed so that boundaries are permanently identifiable on the ground or can be found by simple means.

#### Art. 16

**Date**

<sup>1</sup> As a rule, the boundary markers must be placed before the boundaries are surveyed for the first time.[^16-1]

<sup>2</sup>

Individual boundary markers may be placed after the data is recorded in accordance with paragraph 1:

- a. in the course of updating, unless the boundary was established by inspecting the location;
- b. if there are compelling reasons why it was not possible or expedient to carry out the work earlier.

<sup>3</sup> The boundary markers that are missing in accordance with paragraph 2 must be placed as soon as circumstances allow.

[^16-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 17

**Exceptions**

<sup>1</sup> If boundaries are defined by natural or artificial limits that are permanent and clearly identifiable, monumentation is generally not required.

<sup>2</sup>

The cantons may provide for further exceptions, in particular:

- a. in areas where properties and distinct and permanent rights defined in area are made subject to consolidation;
- b.[^17-1] for properties and distinct and permanent rights defined in area whose boundary markers are continually threatened by agricultural activity or other encroachments;
- c.[^17-2] on agricultural and forest land in mountainous and summer pasture areas as defined in the agricultural production register, and in unproductive areas.

[^17-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 7 March 2003, in force since 1 April 2003 ([AS 2003 507](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2003/111)).
[^17-2]: Amended by No I of the O of 7 March 2003, in force since 1 April 2003 ([AS 2003 507](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2003/111)).

### Section 1 General Provisions

#### Art. 18

**Definitions**

<sup>1</sup> An initial survey is the production of the components of official cadastral surveying in areas that have not been previously subject to a fully approved cadastral survey and in areas defined in Article 51 paragraphs 3 and 4.

<sup>2</sup> Finalisation is defined as the revision or expansion of a fully recognised official cadastral survey in order to adapt it to current regulations.[^18-1]

<sup>3</sup> Updating is the modification of the components of official cadastral surveying to reflect changes in the legal or factual circumstances.

### Section 2 Updating

#### Art. 22

**Principle of updating**

All components of the official cadastral survey are subject to updating.

#### Art. 23

**Continuous updating**[^23-1]

<sup>1</sup> In cases where a reporting system for updating can be organised, the components of the official cadastral survey shall be updated within one year of a change occurring.

<sup>2</sup> The cantons may, after consulting the Federal Directorate of Cadastral Surveying, provide for different time limits in justified cases.

<sup>3</sup> They shall regulate the reporting system.

[^23-1]: Amended by No I of the O of 23 Aug. 2023, in force since 1 Jan. 2024 ([AS 2023 529](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2023/529)).

#### Art. 24

**Periodic updating**

<sup>1</sup> All data that is not subject to continuous updating shall be updated periodically.

<sup>2</sup> Each periodic updating process must cover a large area forming a cohesive whole.

<sup>3</sup> The periodic updating cycle should, if possible, correspond to that of the National Land Survey. It shall not exceed twelve years. The DDPS shall regulate the details of updating.[^24-1]

### Section 4 Objection Procedure, Approval and Compensation

#### Art. 27

#### Art. 28

**Public inspection procedure**[^28-1]

<sup>1</sup> After completion of an initial survey or the finalisation of an official cadastral survey and the elimination of discrepancies in accordance with Article 14*a* where the rights of landowners are affected, a public inspection procedure with objection procedures shall be arranged.[^28-2]

<sup>2</sup> The subject of the public inspection procedure is the land register plan for the area concerned and other extracts from the official cadastral survey data relating to the maintenance of the land register.

<sup>3</sup>

The cantons shall regulate the procedure, subject to the following principles:

- a. The public inspection procedure lasts for 30 days.
- b. Official notice is given of the inspection procedure.
- c.[^28-3] Property owners whose addresses are known shall in addition be informed by standard post of the public inspection procedure and of the legal remedies available to them.
- [tab] d.[^28-4] On request, the landowner shall be provided with an extract from the plan for the land register relating to his or her property in accordance with Article 7 paragraph 2 letters a-c.
- e. An appeal against the objection decision may be filed with a cantonal authority; the authority shall review the decision without restriction.
- f. A final appeal at cantonal level may be made to a court in accordance with Article 75 paragraph 2 of the Federal Supreme Court Act of 17 June 2005[^28-5].

<sup>4</sup> The cantons may stipulate that the public inspection procedure be conducted and official notice thereof be given exclusively by electronic means.[^28-6]

**Approval**[^29-1]

<sup>1</sup> After completion of the public inspection procedure and after first instance objections have been dealt with, the competent cantonal authority shall approve the cadastral survey data, the extracts derived from them and, in particular, the land register plan, irrespective of any disputes yet to be resolved by court action, provided that the data meet the technical and quality requirements under federal law.[^29-2]

<sup>2</sup> This approval confers the probative value of official public documents on the cadastral survey work.

**Principle**

<sup>1</sup> Any person may have access to the official cadastral survey data on demand, in accordance with the regulations in Articles 10–13 GeoIA.

## Calculating the federal contribution

[^t-1]: Inserted by No II of the O of 23 Nov. 2022, in force since 1 Jan. 2023 ([AS 2022 764](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/oc/2022/764)).

When calculating the federal contribution to the funding for cantonal projects in accordance with Article 47*c*, the following percentages apply; they determine the share of allowable costs in accordance with Articles 47*d* and 48:

**1. Initial survey:**

- a. for built-up areas and building zones (Zone I): 15 per cent;
- b. for agricultural and forest land in valleys according to the agricultural production register (Zone II): 30 per cent;
- c. for agricultural and forest land in mountainous and summer pasture areas according to the agricultural production register (Zone III): 45 per cent.

**2. New survey**

If a survey that was carried out in accordance with the regulations that applied before 10 June 1919 is replaced, the values in Number 1 apply.

**3. Finalisation:**

- a. for built-up areas and building zones (Zone I): 15 per cent;
- b. for agricultural and forest land in valleys according to the agricultural production register (Zone II): 20 per cent;
- c. for agricultural and forest land in mountainous and summer pasture areas according to the agricultural production register (Zone III): 35 per cent;
- d. in the case of overall improvements and land reallotments in agriculture and forestry, provided the Confederation does not make payments for the same purpose under other legislation and provided these costs are not borne by third parties: 25 per cent.

**4. Marking**

Marking of territorial and property boundaries for agricultural and forest land in mountainous and summer pasture areas according to the agricultural production register (Zone III), provided the canton bears an appropriate share of the costs: 25 per cent.

**5. Measures following natural events**

For measures carried out following natural events or permanent landslip incidents and which are equivalent to an initial survey, the rates for an initial survey and marking apply.

**6. Special adjustments and periodical updating:**

- a. for special adjustments of exceptional national interest, provided the canton demonstrates that the funding is guaranteed: 60 per cent;
- b. for the costs of periodical updates that are not borne by the person responsible and the funding of which according to information from the canton is guaranteed, for each period in accordance with Article 24 paragraph 3: 60 per cent.

**7. Pilot projects**

Innovative pilot projects to develop official cadastral surveying and to test new technologies: 50–90 per cent, depending on the innovative content and the federal interest.
